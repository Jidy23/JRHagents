# Academic research and quantitative thesis

Use alongside RESEARCH.md and DATA.md when the requested result is a thesis chapter, literature matrix, instrument, or statistical interpretation.

1. Define population, unit of analysis, setting, period, variables and research question. Keep observed facts, author claims, and proposed hypotheses distinct.
2. For a literature matrix record author/year, question, design, sample, measures, method, finding, limitation, and relevance. Verify bibliographic metadata in the actual source. Never fabricate papers, DOI, quotations, or statistics.
3. Align theory → mechanism → measurable indicators → estimand → model → interpretation. Define constructs and coding before analysis; avoid changing hypotheses in response to p-values.
4. Check instrument validity and reliability using methods appropriate to the scale and design. Do not treat Cronbach alpha alone as proof of construct validity.
5. For OLS inspect missingness, influential observations, functional form, dependence, heteroskedasticity, and collinearity as applicable. Robust standard errors address inference under heteroskedasticity; they do not repair biased coefficients caused by confounding or misspecification.
6. For panels, time series, or SEM/PLS, specify the identification and diagnostics appropriate to that design. Do not apply every classical test indiscriminately.
7. Report coefficient, units, uncertainty, sample, model, and substantive meaning. Explain why the sign and magnitude are plausible in real economic behavior. A non-significant estimate is not evidence that the effect equals zero.
8. Distinguish association from causal effects. Explain policy implications only within the evidence and design. Cite using the requested academic style after confirming each source.

If data or output are absent, provide an exact analysis procedure and an interpretation template labeled NOT RUN. Never invent a regression table.
