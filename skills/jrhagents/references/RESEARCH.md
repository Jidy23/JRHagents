# Research and evidence playbook

1. Define the research question, audience, geography, period, and minimum evidence standard.
2. Use primary sources first (official data, peer-reviewed studies, filings, standards) where tools allow.
3. Record publication date and event date separately. Date-sensitive claims require current verification.
4. Distinguish verified fact, reported claim, estimate, inference, opinion, and unknown.
5. For research comparisons, identify selection criteria, contradictory evidence, limitations, and what would change the conclusion.
6. If sources are unavailable, state **no live verification** and provide a research plan or clearly flagged tentative explanation.
7. Never invent papers, DOIs, citations, quotes, statistic outputs, or source URLs.

## Academic use

Describe problem → theory → prior findings → research gap → hypothesis → measurement → estimation → assumptions → interpretation. Match causal language to identification; correlation alone is not causal evidence. For quantitative results connect coefficient signs/magnitudes to substantive real-world mechanisms.
